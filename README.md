# levi-games

Levi's game site — [levi.games](https://levi.games)

A static collection of small browser games made for/by Levi. Hosted via GitHub Pages (see `CNAME`).

## Games

- **Levi Leap** (`pixel-jump/`) — endless runner. Jump (and double-jump) over obstacles, collect coins.
- **Levi Snake** (`snake/`) — classic snake with bonus snacks worth extra points.
- **Levi Crossing** (`levi-crossing/`) — Frogger-style arcade starring the Levi photo sprite. Hop across roads, rivers, and railways through 10 hand-built levels: ride logs, turtle packs (some dive!), and crocodile backs (avoid the snapping head), dodge trains with warning signals, slip across ice, survive night levels lit only by headlights, fog, and croc-infested goal homes — with an eagle that snatches you when the timer runs out. Coins, a shield power-up, and a level-select for every level you've unlocked (progress and best score in `localStorage`).
- **Atom Builder** (`atom-builder/`) — educational sandbox in 3D. Add protons, neutrons, and electrons to build atoms; drag to spin the atom, scroll to zoom. The game identifies the element, isotope, and ion charge. Zoom in on the nucleus to see the quarks (uud / udd) inside protons and neutrons, take the Electron School mini-lessons, and build neutral atoms to discover all 20 elements in the badge collection (saved to `localStorage`).
- **Solar System** (`solar-system/`) — interactive 3D map of the planets. Click any planet (or the Sun) for facts, speed up time to watch orbits, toggle real-distance scale, follow a planet, and take a planet-spotting quiz. Planets spin with cartoon surface detail (Earth's continents and clouds, Mars's polar caps, Neptune's dark spot, Pluto's heart) and carry their moons (the Moon, Phobos & Deimos, the four Galilean moons, Titan). **Missions** mode is a real orbital-mechanics game on top of the comet sandbox: fly 8 probe missions launched from wherever Earth is right now, each with a fuel budget (max launch speed) so the Sun's gravity has to bend the path — hit Mars, Venus, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury and Pluto, skim the Sun like Parker Solar Probe, and put a probe into a full orbit. Fewer attempts = more stars (progress saved in `localStorage`). Synth sound with mute, and keyboard shortcuts (Space pause, 1–4 speed, L labels, T trails, R reset view, M missions, C comets, Q quiz, S sound, Esc back).
- **Squishy Pets** (`squishy-pets/`) — cozy idle collector. Open themed blind boxes, squish soft-body pets to earn Dough, buy upgrades, and collect all 32 pets across 4 categories (Dumpling, Sushi, Sweet, Critter). Progress saves to `localStorage`.
- **Brick Breaker** (`brick-breaker/`) — classic paddle-and-ball brick breaker where the spinning Levi sprite IS the ball. Six level layouts (checkerboard, pyramid, fortress…), two-hit bricks with cracks, falling power-ups (wide paddle, triple ball, slow-mo, extra life), combo pitch on brick streaks, synth sound with mute, and endless faster loops after you beat level 6. Best score persists.
- **Watchmaker** (`watchmaker/`) — a watchmaker school in 3D. Every build is a **commission** from watch history: a **Railroad Pocket Watch (1893)** with the crown at 12, small seconds, a dial with every minute numbered and a bow & chain; a **Trench Watch (1917)** with wire lugs and a red 12; a **Pilot's Watch (1940)** with an onion crown and a triangle at 12; a **Moon-phase Dress Watch (1941)** whose 59-tooth moon disc shows tonight's real Moon; a **Dive Watch (1953)** with crown guards, lume and a one-way rotating bezel; and a **Moon Chronograph (1969)** with three sub-dials, a tachymeter, pushers and column-wheel chronograph works. Each type has its own case, crown, printed dial, hands and strap, and the complications add real extra parts to the build. Pick a **craftsmanship grade** — Standard (7 jewels), Fine (17 jewels, blued screws) or Chronometer (21 jewels in gold chatons, a screw balance, and the real −4/+6 s/day chronometer standard); Fine and Chronometer movements are **hand-finished** first by cutting Côtes de Genève stripes into the plate, and your stripes become the plate's actual surface in 3D. The movement build is true to a real one: after the barrel, train, escape wheel and pallet fork come the **bridges** (holding the upper pivots), the **ratchet wheel & click**, the balance and the **balance cock** with its shock jewel and regulator — then you **oil the jewels** (hold for a drop, let go in the band; too much floods). A seventh type, the **Self-winding Watch (1931)**, adds a **rotor** seen swinging through an open-heart window in the dial, and is wound by swinging the watch instead of the crown (and can't be overwound, thanks to its slipping bridle). A career layer ties it together: every build, repair and quiz earns **money and Guild reputation**; the commission board carries **customer orders** (type, grade and style wishes, paid with a bonus if matched); the **Guild** shows your rank (Trainee → Craftsman → Master Craftsman → Guild Master → Living Legend) and sells **workshop tools** (loupe, tweezers, oiler, bench lamp) that genuinely widen the game's zones. **The Finishing Room**: Fine and Chronometer builds are finished by hand with real techniques, each leaving its mark on the 3D watch — Côtes de Genève stripes (bridges, cock and rotor), **perlage** circular graining (mainplate), **heat-bluing** the screws on a brass tray (quench at cornflower blue, 290–300 °C; straw, brown and purple come first, overshoot and they go grey), **anglage** (tracing the bridge edges with a file to bevel and polish them) and **Zaratsu** (holding the case dead steady against a spinning tin plate for a distortion-free mirror); bezels and lugs are mirror-polished, bands brushed, and a studio environment gives all the metal real reflections. An eighth type, the **Snowflake Glide Watch (1999)**, is a Grand Seiko–inspired Spring Drive: no escapement at all — a glide wheel braked by a magnet and checked by a quartz regulator, so the blued seconds hand sweeps with no tick, a power-reserve arc, a snow-textured dial, diamond-cut hands and markers, a Magic Lever, and ±1 s/day on a timegrapher that draws one smooth line. The Parts Guide ends with a connoisseur's guide to finishing (black polish, guilloché, hairline brushing…). **Full Service** is the restorer's mode: a customer brings in a tired heirloom (any type, 25–70 years old, with a story) that is tarnished, dust-stained, scratched and gummed with dried oil, and can barely swing its balance. You unscrew the bezel with the case wrench, strip every part off in reverse order into the tray (last on, first off — the game tells you why when you grab the wrong one), inspect the tray and decide which worn parts to replace (a set mainspring, a kinked hairspring, a perished gasket…) and which just need cleaning, load the ultrasonic machine — choosing what goes in and what is brushed by hand, because the ultrasonic strips paint and lume off a dial — then rebuild, oil, wind and regulate it. The service report shows a before/after portrait, stars, the bill, and what the customer says. A ninth watch, the **Tourbillon (1801)**, is unlocked at Guild rank Master Craftsman: its build swaps the escape wheel, pallet fork, balance and cock for a single **tourbillon cage** that carries all three round once a minute behind a window at 6 o'clock, its blued arrow serving as the seconds hand. The start screen is grouped into Learn, Build a Watch, The Workshop and Your Career. **The Bench** is a sandbox for just looking: pick any of the 8 watches at any grade and finish, spin it, **explode** it apart on a slider (every part lifts in build order), slow the escapement down to 1/50 speed or freeze it, **X-ray** through the dial and case, tap any part (on the model or in the list) to highlight it and read what it does, view it alone, follow the **power flow** from mainspring to hands, and change the colours live. A **Watch History** timeline (1510 to 1999 — Huygens' balance spring, Mudge's lever escapement, Harrison's H4, Breguet's tourbillon, railroad standards, the Moon landing, the quartz crisis…) comes with a history quiz, and the Showcase tracks your collection of every type at every grade. Start with the **Lesson**, a guided tour of a running watch that lights up each part and follows the power from mainspring to hands; browse the **Parts Guide** (every part with its role and a real-horology fact); and test yourself with **Name That Part**. Then build: 12 parts in their proper order — mainplate, barrel & mainspring, gear train, escape wheel, pallet fork, balance wheel, dial, hands, then case, crown & stem, crystal and strap. Three tiers: **Apprentice** (the bench shows what goes next and why), **Journeyman** (parts shuffled — reason out the order; wrong picks explain why), and **Master** (**no labels** — identify every part from its rendered shape alone, fast needle, no rate readout). Each part is graded on placement precision (bullseye on its slot) and then **seated** with a timing gauge. The finished watch has no power until you **wind** it (hold or circle the crown, let go in the gold band — overwind and the spring slips); then it comes alive, and you **regulate** it on a live timegrapher, **set it to the real time** by turning the crown (the hands then keep real time), and **case it up** in your own case metal, dial, hands and strap. Scored results with ranks, per-tier best scores, and a **Showcase** gallery of every watch you've finished. Then open the **Repair Shop**: five customers a day bring in broken watches (snapped mainspring, magnetised hairspring, a limping out-of-beat tick, a loose minute hand, a fogged crystal…). Each fault shows up in the running 3D movement — stopped, racing, stuttering or limping, with the tick-tock to match — and in the customer's own words. Inspect parts with the loupe (first look free, extra looks cost), fix the one you think is broken (or call it without looking for a Sharp Eye bonus), then test it on the timegrapher and get paid; every job ends with what was wrong and the clue that gave it away. Best day and lifetime repairs are saved. Three.js r128 is vendored locally, so it works offline like the rest of the site.
- **Levi Galaxy** (`levi-galaxy/`) — Galaga-style space shooter. Levi rides a rocket board and auto-fires at formations of alien invaders (drones, crabs that shoot, zig-zagging wasps, armored tanks) that swoop in, sway, and dive-bomb him. Every 5th wave is a Mothership boss with bullet rings, aimed spreads, and escort drones. Asteroids drift in from wave 3. Catch power-ups (spread shot, rapid fire, shield, bombs, extra lives), drop bombs to clear the screen, and chain kills for a score multiplier. Keyboard, mouse, or touch; best score persists.
- **Levi Dig** (`levi-dig/`) — a digging adventure. Levi digs straight down through real rock layers — topsoil, clay, sandstone, limestone, granite, basalt and obsidian — each with a geology fact when you reach it, and gets darker the deeper he goes (only his helmet lamp lights the way). Hold a direction to dig, climb by placing ladders, collect ores and gems (copper to diamond), and ride the rope back to camp to sell them and buy upgrades: pickaxe → power drill → diamond drill (needed for the harder layers), lunchbox energy, backpack, lamp, hard hat, gas mask and a fossil radar. Hazards: boulders that wobble and fall, hidden gas pockets, and flowing lava; run out of energy and the rescue team hauls you up minus half your bag. 17 fossils and treasures (arrowhead, mammoth tooth, megalodon tooth, mosquito in amber, trilobite, meteorite, a 4-piece T. rex…) twinkle in the dark and go into Levi's Museum with a fact card each. The goal: the Crystal Cave and its giant crystal, 500 m down. Extra gear: **dynamite** (light it and run — blasts a 3×3 hole, but not obsidian) and **snacks** (+40 energy). **Bats** flit around the caves (only their red eyes show in the dark). Four hidden **landmarks** each come with a true story and a reward: a 1920s time capsule, an abandoned gold mine with timber props, rails, a lantern and a cart of gold, a glowworm grotto, and a lava tube. The camp **job board** offers contracts (deliver ores, survey a depth, find a fossil or a landmark), and the **Rock & Mineral Guide** identifies every rock and mineral you dig with its type, Mohs hardness and a real fact. A fifth landmark, the **Underground Lake**, has real flowing water: Levi floats at the surface, dives for cave pearls on a limited breath (diving gear extends it), and water pours down any shaft dug beneath it, levels out through tunnels, and turns lava to basalt in a burst of steam. New finds are cleaned by hand in a **fossil prep** mini-game (rub the rock away to reveal them), and the museum's **Great Hall** assembles the T. rex skeleton bone by bone. The dark now **remembers** every tile your lamp has lit, and a **map of the dig** (V) shows the whole explored shaft with layers, landmarks found and rumours of the ones still hidden. A **canary** upgrade chirps and marks gas pockets, and there are **16 badges** to earn. The world, progress and museum persist in `localStorage`.

## Run locally

It's all static HTML/CSS/JS — just open `index.html` in a browser, or serve the folder:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

Then visit http://localhost:8000.

## Saves & players

Everything is browser-based with no backend: each game saves to `localStorage` automatically. The home page's **SAVES & PLAYERS** panel adds **players** (several people on one device, each with their own progress in every game — switching swaps the whole save set over), **backup** (download a JSON save file, or copy a gzip-compressed `LEVI1.…` save code to paste on another device), **load** (from a code or a file, with a summary and confirmation first), and **start over**. Profiles are stored as snapshots under `levi.profile.<name>` with `levi.profiles` / `levi.activeProfile`.

## Home page

The root `index.html` has Levi himself bouncing beside the title with a speech bubble, a **Continue** button for the last game played, and a **What's New** board fed by the `UPDATES` array (newest first) that flags anything added since your last visit — add a line there whenever a game changes. It renders the game grid from a `GAMES` array: category filter chips, a **Surprise me** random picker, NEW / UPDATED / LAST PLAYED ribbons, a pixel starfield with parallax and shooting stars, and a per-game readout of your best score / progress pulled from each game's own `localStorage` save (best scores, elements found, pets collected, mission stars, watchmaker rank…).

## Adding a new game

1. Create a new folder at the repo root (e.g. `my-game/`) with an `index.html`.
2. Add an entry to the `GAMES` array in the root `index.html` (folder id, name, emoji, blurb, tag, category, tint). Optionally add a reader to `STATS` so the card shows a best score.
3. Match the existing dark / yellow pixel styling for consistency.

Per-game best scores are persisted in `localStorage`.
